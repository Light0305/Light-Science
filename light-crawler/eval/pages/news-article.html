City Council Approves Riverfront Renewal Plan

By Maria Chen, staff reporter

The city council voted eight to one on Tuesday to approve the Riverfront Renewal Plan, a 240 million dollar programme that will rebuild the eastern embankment over the next four years.

Construction will begin in March 2027 with the demolition of the old ferry terminal, which the plan converts into a public market hall with space for sixty local vendors.

The approved design adds 1,900 metres of new cycling paths and a continuous pedestrian promenade between Bridge Street and the harbour lock.

Mayor Elena Ruiz said the vote closes six years of public consultation, during which the planning office held forty-one neighbourhood meetings.

Opponents on the council argued that the flood-defence budget line remains underfunded by roughly twelve million dollars, a figure the city engineer disputed during the debate.
